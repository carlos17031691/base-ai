## Goal

#140 found that 12 of 27 extra implementer completions came from fixing a ticket's own Low findings after an approved review. About half of them were re-entries with no file change, made only so the coordinator's fixes could be re-reviewed. The human chose option (b) for R5 (2026-11-24): a delta-only re-review with no new implementer completion. That is also #138 F6, which is implemented here only. #140 also found that the planner's reasoning effort is neither set nor recorded (R1), and that plans keep leaving `usage.jsonl` and the package check out of "Files to touch" (R3, #138 F4). This ticket does three things. It sets `model: opus` and `effort: high` in `planner.md` and records the effort in `usage.jsonl` when the transcript gives it. It adds a delta re-review round that is bound to the same implementer completion and passes `pipeline check` with no rule change. It adds the `usage.jsonl` row and the `check:package` step to the plan template and to the planner's guidance.

## Design

**D1 — Planner model and effort (R1).** The frontmatter of `.claude/agents/planner.md` gains `effort: high` after `model: opus`. Nothing else in the frontmatter changes.

**D2 — Effort in `usage.jsonl` (R1).** All in `cli/src/usage.ts`:
1. `Message` gains an optional `effort`. `parseTranscript` takes it from the line's top-level `effort` on the first line of each `message.id`, and only when the value is one of `low`, `medium`, `high`, `xhigh`, `max`. Any other value, or none, leaves it absent. `perTurnEffort` is not read. The value goes into a versioned file, so it is bounded to that enum, as `MESSAGE_ID` bounds the model id.
2. `buildRecords` groups by model and effort, in order of first appearance. A record has `effort` right after `model` when its messages have one. Otherwise the key is absent, never `null` and never `"unknown"`. `schema` stays `1`.
3. Readers do not change. `parseUsageLine` ignores `effort`, so old and new records give the same groups and totals in `usage report` and the dashboard. An old CLI reading a new record also ignores it. The high-water mark does not change: it reads `last_message_id` over all of a session's records.

**D3 — Delta re-review for own-ticket Lows (R5 (b), #138 F6).**
- **Trigger** (new `reviewer.md` Step 1 row). It sits after the "newer implementer completion → Round N+1" row and before the "already reviewed" halt. All of these must hold:
  - `state` is `completed` and the plan is locked;
  - a previous round exists and the latest round is `approved`;
  - there is no newer implementer completion;
  - the invocation asks for a delta re-review and names the Low findings it fixes, by their ids in `review-report.md`.

  A named id that is not a Low finding of the report is a halt.
- **Base.** The newest commit listed under "commits under review" in the latest round, or that round's merge-base when it listed none. If `git merge-base --is-ancestor <base> HEAD` fails, halt. The delta change set is `git diff --name-status <base>`, `git diff <base>` and `git ls-files --others --exclude-standard`. The Step 2 branch check still applies. The development base choice does not, because the delta is not compared with `development`. An empty delta is a halt, as today.
- **The round** goes in `reviewer/re-review.md`, under a heading that starts `## Round <N> re-review (delta)`. It records:
  - the timestamp;
  - the same implementer completion as the round before, which it states is unchanged;
  - the base, HEAD and commits since the base;
  - the Low ids "as given by the invocation";
  - for each named Low: Fixed or Not fixed, with `file:line`;
  - a scope check of the delta files against "Files to touch";
  - a rule for hunks: each hunk is explained by a named Low, or it is a **Medium** ("a change outside the named Low fixes needs a new implementer completion"). The pipeline artefacts in `.ai/tasks/<ticket-id>/` (owners' rounds, `status.json` entries in their permitted fields, `usage.jsonl`) are exempt from this rule;
  - new findings in the delta, with new Lows carrying `To register` as usual;
  - the acceptance-criteria table, one row per criterion. A row whose evidence the delta does not touch carries over as "Met (round <M>; evidence not touched by the delta)". A row whose evidence it touches is traced again;
  - a complete `### Security` section for the delta;
  - the verdict.
- **The Step 8 note** keeps its form. It inserts `delta of round <M> from <base>; ` before `reviewed implementer completion <timestamp>`, so K9, K10 and K18 parse it unchanged.
- **Who fixes.** The coordinator applies the fixes, on the human's instruction, only in files listed in "Files to touch". It commits them with the ticket id and then invokes the reviewer in delta mode. There is no implementer re-entry and no `status.json` change. No `decisions.md` block is needed, because no transition needs covering. This is an exception to coordinator item 5 (H1).
- **QA and the supervisor.** Both treat the delta round as a review round of the same, latest completion:
  - QA runs after it. If no QA round exists, this is QA round 1. Otherwise it is a same-completion re-verification (`qa.md:201`), whose invocation gives "review round <N> delta after own-ticket Low fixes" as the reason. K14 enforces this order.
  - The supervisor validates on the delta round (K10, K11) and on a QA round after it (K14, K15), with no change to its definition.
  - The coordinator runs the delta before QA where possible, so QA runs once.
  - A `changes_requested` delta leads to the ordinary send-back, re-entry and full Round N+1. K18 counts that as an ordinary re-entry, because the delta note names the previous completion.
- **Guards kept:**
  - a delta cannot follow a `changes_requested` round;
  - a delta cannot replace a review of a new completion, because the full row comes first;
  - a delta cannot carry an unexplained change (Medium);
  - a delta cannot touch files outside "Files to touch" (blocking, as in Step 3);
  - QA re-verifies everything after the delta.
